# World Conquest: random continents, exploration, and regional conquest

## Problem Statement

Players want a much larger battlefield where they start with the familiar small force, explore unknown geography, fight for territory, and grow their own military until their nation controls the whole map. Current skirmishes expose the underlying geography and locations, and their capture points or Production Buildings determine victory. Making a map larger alone does not provide the requested conquest loop.

The game must remain a real-time multiplayer match for friends, with flexible human and AI teams. It must retain normal combat, orders, and unit control. It must not become a multi-day campaign, require naval travel, grant a large starting army, or convert defeated enemy troops into the conqueror's army.

## Solution

Add **World Conquest** as a separate lobby mode. Generate a fresh, connected continent for each match, divided into **64 regions for Huge** or **128 regions for Massive**. Each participating player or lobby AI starts with one owned home region and the normal small starting package appropriate to construction and recruitment. Remaining regions contain local defenders and military bases that must be fought over.

Most geography, region locations, resources, military bases, and enemy positions are initially unknown. Players scout to discover the world. Explored ground remains on the map; enemy activity outside current shared vision follows the existing fog and Ghost rules. Every conquerable region must be reachable over land, with bridges or fords across rivers and no required boats.

Conquest has two steps: destroy the region's defending military base, then have infantry claim its capture point. A force may conquer any region it can reach, including territory behind an enemy's front. Claiming land never converts enemy troops or grants a completed Production Building. Players pay to construct their own facilities on suitable ground inside owned territory and train units through their own queues.

Regions provide basic income and gradual population-cap growth. Cities, industrial areas, and resource regions have different additional value. Teammates share territory and its benefits while retaining command of their own units. Local defenders stay near their regions; lobby AI scouts, recruits, expands, and attacks like a player.

Losing the last owned region causes defeat. A team wins only when it owns every region, including formerly unclaimed guarded territory. Aim for **45–60 minutes** on Huge, with Massive and longer-match settings allowing longer games. This is a pacing target, not a timer that awards victory before total conquest.

## User Stories

1. As a host, I want to select World Conquest in the lobby, so that my friends can play the new mode without changing the existing modes.
2. As a host, I want to select Huge or Massive, so that I can choose a 64-region or 128-region world.
3. As a host, I want to configure human players, lobby AI, and teams using the normal lobby, so that we can play free-for-all, friends against friends, co-op against AI, or mixed matches.
4. As a player, I want each match to generate different geography and starting positions, so that scouting teaches me this match's world instead of rewarding memorized locations.
5. As a player, I want every conquerable region connected by ground routes, so that I can reach the whole continent without boats.
6. As a player, I want rivers to have usable bridges or fords, so that crossings create tactical choices without making required territory unreachable.
7. As a player, I want my starting home region and nearby approaches to be usable and reasonably fair, so that a bad generation does not decide the match before combat.
8. As a player, I want meaningful towns, roads, woods, hills, and military positions across the continent, so that its size creates choices rather than long stretches of empty walking.
9. As a player, I want one home region and the normal small starting force, so that I earn expansion instead of spawning with a large military.
10. As a player, I want the normal selection, movement, attack, ability, retreat, and rally controls, so that I can play World Conquest using skills I already know.
11. As a player, I want to command my own squads and vehicles, so that conquest does not introduce autonomous army commanders or automatic battles.
12. As a player, I want unscouted terrain fully concealed, so that exploration reveals roads, crossings, settlements, and resources through actual movement.
13. As a player, I want enemy home regions, military bases, and regional capture points hidden until discovered, so that I must find opponents and useful places.
14. As a player, I want explored ground to remain visible as remembered terrain, so that I can plan routes through places I have already visited.
15. As a player, I want live enemy positions shown only through current shared vision, so that scouting and concealment remain meaningful.
16. As a teammate, I want discoveries shared within my team, so that we can coordinate exploration and attacks.
17. As a player, I want the lobby preview, minimap, map view, and reconnect messages to respect discovery, so that those surfaces do not reveal the world early.
18. As a player, I want movement into unknown ground to reveal obstacles when encountered, so that displayed routes or immediate command errors do not disclose hidden geography.
19. As a player facing AI, I want AI opponents to discover the world through the same information available to players, so that they cannot march directly to an unseen home region.
20. As a player, I want unclaimed regions defended by local hostile forces, so that discovering new land leads to combat before I encounter another participating player.
21. As a player, I want local defenders to defend their own regions rather than expand across the continent, so that their role differs from lobby AI opponents.
22. As a player, I want lobby AI to recruit, build, explore, and conquer, so that an AI nation competes for the map rather than acting as a stationary guard.
23. As an attacker, I want a region's defending military base to block capture while it survives, so that conquest requires destruction before occupation.
24. As an attacker, I want infantry to claim the regional capture point after the base falls, so that destroying a structure alone does not immediately grant ownership.
25. As a defender, I want nearby hostile infantry to contest claims through the normal capture rules, so that I can interrupt an occupation.
26. As a player, I want to conquer any reachable region without an adjacency requirement, so that scouting, flanking, and attacks behind enemy lines are useful.
27. As a player, I want surviving hostile troops to remain hostile after a claim, so that no enemy army becomes mine automatically.
28. As a player, I want conquered military facilities to require my own rebuilding and paid recruitment, so that expansion rewards investment rather than free units.
29. As a player, I want to place facilities on any suitable ground inside owned territory, so that the old enemy installation does not dictate my construction layout.
30. As a player, I want normal construction costs, Engineers, prerequisites, and Construction Sites, so that new bases use familiar mechanics.
31. As a player, I want to train units at my own finished Production Buildings, so that newly recruited units belong to me and remain under my control.
32. As a player, I want recruitment to respect resources, queue limits, and population capacity, so that territorial growth does not bypass the existing economy.
33. As a player, I want basic income from each owned region, so that expansion funds further construction and recruitment.
34. As a player, I want population capacity to grow gradually with owned regions, so that I can support a larger military after earning more territory.
35. As a player, I want cities, industrial regions, and resource regions to have distinct bonuses, so that the next target has value beyond its location.
36. As a teammate, I want regional ownership and bonuses shared with my team, so that a teammate's conquest benefits our joint effort.
37. As a teammate, I want my unit selection, production queues, and army commands to remain my own, so that shared territory does not merge everyone's controls.
38. As a player, I want loss of territory to remove its future income and capacity contribution, so that opponents can weaken my expansion by recapturing land.
39. As a player, I want existing units to survive a reduced population cap, so that losing a region does not silently delete soldiers I already recruited.
40. As a player, I want to keep fighting after losing my original HQ while my side still owns territory, so that the agreed last-region defeat rule permits a comeback.
41. As a player, I want a paid route to restore construction and recruitment after production loss, so that surviving territory remains useful even after my original HQ is destroyed.
42. As a player, I want retreat, reinforcement, rallying, and the home shortcut to use valid friendly locations, so that they do not send my forces to a conquered enemy-held home.
43. As a player, I want clear feedback when a base is destroyed, a claim is contested, a region is gained, or a region is lost, so that I can understand the changing front.
44. As a player, I want discovered borders and my side's region count visible in the map views, so that I can track progress without exposing undiscovered enemy territory.
45. As a player, I want defeat to follow loss of the last owned region, so that neither original-HQ loss nor temporary production loss ends my match prematurely.
46. As a team, I want victory only after owning every region, so that defeating the other participating nations does not skip the remaining guarded territory.
47. As a player, I want the result to show my side controlling the continent, so that total conquest delivers the intended nation-owner victory.
48. As a player, I want a match paced for an evening rather than a multi-day campaign, so that I can complete it with friends in one session.
49. As a returning player, I want same-match reconnect to restore only my team's known world and current territory, so that reconnect preserves play without revealing hidden locations.
50. As a player, I want either preset to keep orders, camera movement, and multiplayer updates responsive, so that a large world remains playable as armies grow.
51. As a player of the existing modes, I want their maps, combat balance, victory rules, and lobby behavior preserved, so that World Conquest adds a choice rather than replacing the normal game.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Separate mode:** introduce World Conquest in lobby configuration, room creation, authoritative match setup, client mode presentation, and match results. Keep the existing Conquest, Assault, Annihilation, Classic, and Horde rules intact.
- **Vocabulary:** a region is a bounded territorial area with an identifier, a regional military base or control installation, an infantry capture point, a type, rewards, and ownership. A home region is a participating player's initial owned region. A nation is a player side's territorial identity, not a new unit roster or government simulation. Use HQ, Engineers, Production Buildings, Construction Sites, manpower (MP), Fuel, Munitions, population cap, shared vision, Ghost, and Command Card in their established game meanings.
- **World generation:** generate the authoritative world server-side with a fresh seed at match start. Store seed and preset for reproduction, but do not reveal information that reconstructs the hidden geography during a live match. Size means region count, not cell dimensions. Physical dimensions and terrain density must support the intended travel and combat pacing and pass the performance checks.
- **Connectivity and fairness:** validate that every region and its capture point can be reached by ordinary ground forces. Use connected roads and terrain, usable infantry and vehicle approaches, bridges and fords, suitable home construction space, and comparable starting access to expansion. Required regions must retain a ground route when destructible bridges are lost; durable crossings or alternative land routes prevent permanent isolation. Do not generate detached conquerable islands or require Shipyards.
- **Regions are distinct from legacy Points:** do not represent 64 or 128 regions by blindly expanding the existing small capture-point array. Define mode-specific regional state and validation while reusing normal capture behavior at regional capture points. Existing map formats and limits remain valid for existing modes.
- **Starting package:** reuse the existing small construction-capable Classic package, a finished HQ, an Engineer Squad, and a Rifle Squad, as the technical interpretation of the agreed normal start. Do not increase starting squad counts with world size or pre-grant armies from other regions. Existing faction choices and eligible units continue to apply.
- **Regional control:** the server tracks regional ownership, the required defending installations, claim progress, the claiming side, and region type. A region becomes claimable only after its defending military base and defending owner's live Production Buildings in the region have been destroyed. Infantry then claims it using normal capture radius, timing, and contesting rules. The same destruction-and-claim sequence applies to recapture. Destruction without infantry occupation does not change ownership.
- **No conquest transfers:** regional claims never change the owner of surviving hostile units or grant completed Production Buildings. Destroyed facilities remain wrecks or rubble. Non-production facilities do not automatically become the new owner's assets or continue paying their former owner territorial income. Recruit and rebuild through normal paid mechanisms.
- **Ownership and teams:** in free-for-all, each player's side owns its regions. In team matches, regional ownership and benefits belong to the team, while units, Production Buildings, queues, and resource spending remain player-controlled. Each participating player contributes one home region at setup. Losing the team's last region defeats that team and its remaining seats; a member is not eliminated merely because their original home region or Production Buildings were lost while shared team territory survives. This resolves the agreed shared-territory and last-region rules together.
- **Construction and recovery:** add owned-territory checks to normal placement validation, alongside vision, terrain, footprint, cost, prerequisites, and Engineer requirements. Construction is allowed on suitable owned ground, not only on the destroyed military-base footprint. Preserve a paid route to restore basic construction and recruitment when a surviving side has lost all Production Buildings or its original Engineer source. There must be no dependency dead end that effectively defeats a side before its last region is lost. Recovery must not create free squads or convert enemy facilities.
- **Regional economy:** each region supplies a configured basic income contribution and population-cap contribution. Cities, industrial areas, and resource regions provide additional documented bonuses through existing resources or production behavior. Apply team-derived benefits through deterministic sharing among active teammates. Ownership changes remove or add contributions exactly once; claim races, reconnects, or repeated packets cannot duplicate rewards. Keep player resource balances and paid recruitment familiar.
- **Population and queues:** grow capacity gradually from the normal starting limit. Define finite growth coefficients and ceilings in mode tuning. Existing units and committed queues count through normal population accounting. If territory loss puts a player above capacity, retain existing units and block additional capacity use until legal again. Do not delete units or bypass queue/resource checks as a capacity adjustment.
- **True discovery:** authoritative terrain, heights, region geometry, capture-point coordinates, Resource Nodes, enemy HQ locations, hostile structures, guard locations, and hidden ownership changes must be filtered by the recipient team's knowledge. During a live match, the lobby preview, public room metadata, map-download surfaces, initial messages, snapshots, reconnect messages, minimap, map view, and spectator views must not provide a second route around discovery. Public settings, teams, factions, preset region counts, and a side's own territorial totals may remain visible.
- **Remembered world:** maintain separate current-vision and explored knowledge per team. Unknown terrain uses a transport representation distinct from actual authoritative terrain. Previously explored terrain and building Ghosts follow remembered-state rules; unseen destruction or enemy movement cannot update them early. Reconnect restores that team's knowledge without revealing the full generated world. Teammates share discoveries. Complete world reveal is appropriate after the match is decided.
- **Movement and hidden information:** retain normal move and attack-move orders, but do not expose route waypoints through unseen obstacles or immediately reject unknown destinations based on private terrain. Treat unexplored ground as uncertain and reveal or replan obstacles when encountered. The server still enforces physical movement and collision. Do not introduce automated army commanders or separate battle resolution.
- **AI and local defenders:** lobby AI plans through the same filtered observations and public command interface as a human, explores unknown frontiers, discovers regional targets, destroys bases, occupies capture points, and constructs/recruits in its own territory. Remove enemy-spawn fallbacks for this mode. Local defenders are hostile region-bound forces, not additional competitive lobby seats. They may react and fight locally but do not conquer, grow nations, or explore the continent. Defeated guards are not granted to the claimant.
- **Defeat and victory:** regional ownership is the source of truth. No original-HQ elimination, production-count elimination, VP race, last-opponent shortcut, or Classic Sudden Death determines World Conquest results. A side loses its last region and is defeated. A side wins when it owns all generated regions, including former local-defender territory. Continue normal multiplayer result delivery and return-to-lobby behavior after resolution. Army ownership must never transfer to a conquering enemy side.
- **Presentation and scale:** extend the normal Command Card, map views, borders, population display, and Alerts to show only discovered regional information, claim prerequisites, ownership changes, and regional benefits. Preserve normal unit selection and battle controls. Treat terrain generation, geometry, textures, fog updates, pathfinding, AI, and network payloads as bounded work; use chunking, incremental updates, or other measured improvements where needed. A larger validation limit alone is not evidence of a playable preset.
- **Pacing and balance:** 64 and 128 are the accepted preset targets, not measured support claims. Aim for 45–60 minutes on Huge and longer selectable play on Massive. Tune guard strength, starting access, travel distances, income, capacity growth, and region bonuses through representative matches. Record actual measurements and balance numbers in the project's design record and changelog. Do not silently shrink the agreed presets to make a performance claim pass.

## Testing Decisions

- **Primary seam, confirmed by the user:** use the existing real WebSocket room harness. Real clients join a room, configure the lobby, issue normal commands, advance controlled time, and inspect their received start messages, snapshots, Alerts, and match results. This is the highest existing seam that covers authoritative outcomes and information disclosure together. Prefer this one seam over a separate test framework for every new internal module.
- **Good tests:** assert externally observable behavior and illegal-action rejection, not generator loops, private arrays, helper call counts, or copied implementation formulas. Use deterministic generated fixtures and a controlled clock for reproducibility. Existing room, lobby, fogged-building, reconnect, command, queue, and endgame tests provide prior art. Small fixtures cover transitions; full-size worlds establish the actual preset counts and scale.
- **Generation and setup acceptance:** verify fresh-world setup, the exact 64/128 region counts, one home region per participating player or lobby AI, the normal small starting package, and ordinary ground access to all required regions. Include invalid/disconnected generation rejection, fair starting construction/expansion access, and a crossing-loss fixture that retains a conquerable land route. Regions must not consume additional human lobby seats.
- **Discovery acceptance:** inspect all initial and reconnect payloads for absence of unknown terrain, heights, regional sites, Resource Nodes, enemy spawn coordinates, guards, and unrevealed ownership changes. Scout, lose sight, and return to check current vision, remembered terrain, and Ghost behavior. Exercise teammate knowledge sharing, spectator restrictions, public metadata/map endpoints, route disclosure, and commands toward unknown obstacles. Perturb private unseen state while preserving observations and require unchanged player-visible output and AI decisions until discovery.
- **Conquest and recruitment acceptance:** a surviving military base prevents a claim; its destruction alone does not grant a region; infantry occupation completes a legal claim; contested capture does not complete. Repeat for recapture and a non-adjacent reachable region. Verify no enemy unit or finished Production Building changes to the conquering owner. Verify paid construction on suitable owned ground, rejection elsewhere, prerequisites, queue limits, resource charges, unit ownership, and actual recruitment completion.
- **Teams, economy, and recovery acceptance:** assert shared territory and regional benefits without duplicate payments, individual unit/queue control, gradual capacity growth, loss of income/capacity on recapture, and preservation of units above a reduced cap. Destroy the original HQ and all production while another owned region survives, then use the paid recovery route to rebuild and recruit. A member with no original home remains active while team regions survive; retreat and home navigation use valid friendly locations.
- **Defenders and endgame acceptance:** verify local defenders stay regional and lobby AI expands from its observed world. Losing the last region produces defeat; losing only the original HQ or production does not. Owning fewer than all regions cannot win even after every rival participating side is defeated. Owning all regions produces the correct team result. Crossing the old Classic deadline or the pacing target cannot trigger Sudden Death or a premature win.
- **Supporting browser checks:** verify mode/preset selection, ordinary controls, opaque unknown terrain, explored ground, discovered borders and locations, claim feedback, region bonuses, construction/recruitment, team counters, reconnect, and the result view with real browser clients. Existing water/fog rendering-adapter tests support renderer regressions; they do not substitute for a whole-world gameplay acceptance test.
- **Performance and balance evidence:** profile representative Huge and Massive matches at supported player counts, with grown armies and local defenders, through real server/client behavior. Record hardware, runtime, generation/start latency, simulation tick latency, long movement-command latency, AI cost, payload size, memory, and browser frame timing. Exercise distant travel, region captures, terrain changes, reconnect, and match cleanup. Seeded AI runs and human multiplayer sessions measure pacing and faction/spawn effects. Existing simulation/path and browser-rendering performance work is prior art, not proof that either new preset already works.
- **Regression requirements:** run the required general game regression suite before any implementation commit and the world-rendering checks when renderer/fog/terrain changes apply. Preserve regression coverage for the existing modes, lobby admission, reconnect, construction, population accounting, visibility, and match results. This issue defines future verification; creating the spec does not claim those checks have run against an implementation.

## Out of Scope

- Replacing the existing modes or changing their victory rules and combat balance.
- Multi-day campaign persistence, offline progress, scheduled turns, or a weeks-long world.
- Naval conquest, detached required islands, new boats, or mandatory Shipyards.
- Larger starting armies, free captured troops, automatic enemy army conversion, or free completed recruitment facilities.
- Autonomous army commanders, automatic battle resolution, or a separate turn-based strategy layer.
- Elections, government management, laws, diplomacy, or a political simulation. Owning the continent is the victory outcome.
- New faction rosters, technology trees, or unit redesigns unrelated to enabling the agreed territorial mode.
- Guaranteed frame rates, match lengths, or supported physical dimensions without live measurements.

## Further Notes

- This spec synthesizes the user's accepted design decisions. The user confirmed the testing boundary and explicitly requested implementation after tracker publication. This issue is the implementation contract; publication itself does not establish that the feature is built or verified.
- The visual direction is the approved Continental Conquest concept revised into one connected mainland, including the former western island region. The exploration concept illustrates later progress, not a large initial army. Generated artwork is a design reference, not authoritative terrain, a literal continent-distance requirement, or evidence of an implemented feature.
- Construction-capable Classic starting units, team-level defeat interpretation, construction recovery, recapture requirements, and discovery-safe protocol behavior are technical clarifications needed to make the accepted rules coherent. Numeric income, guard, capacity, sharing, region-type, and travel tuning remains implementation work to document and test, not a new design interview.
- Existing source has ordinary small starting forces, Classic construction/recruitment, shared vision, building Ghosts, seeded offline map generation, real multiplayer protocol tests, and six-seat lobby support. It does not currently implement regions, complete hidden-terrain delivery, region-based capacity, last-region elimination, or total territorial victory. Do not treat either preset as already supported.
- The current map validator allows up to 1024 cells per axis and only nine legacy capture points. Terrain rendering retains substantial per-cell data. World Conquest must address regional schema and scaling rather than merely raising a number or broadcasting the entire generated world.
- Current start messages provide the complete map and all participant spawn positions. Classic has a 25-minute Sudden Death path and production-loss elimination, and its AI can fall back to enemy spawn positions. These behaviors need mode-specific replacements for World Conquest; the existing modes retain them.
- Update the domain glossary, design record, and changelog alongside implementation. Use current source for baseline values where historical documentation differs. Record measurements honestly, and keep unresolved performance or balance limits visible until the final checks establish completion.
