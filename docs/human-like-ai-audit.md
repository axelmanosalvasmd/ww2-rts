# Human-like commander: source audit

Audited 2026-10-04 against `fc60be93907b9214a6699f4e88753180c9422dea`, before implementation. References below describe that revision. They must be rechecked after code moves. This audit covers player-seat commanders, including co-op teammates and human handovers. Horde movement has a separate batching exception.

## The eight claims in the brief

| Claim | Finding and exact source | What the replacement must change |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A look issues every action in one tick. | Confirmed for accepted commands from one invocation. `think()` passes a synchronous `command()` handle at `shared/ai.js:239`; `submit` immediately sends at `shared/ai.js:250-251`. Economy runs at `shared/ai.js:298`, purchases at `shared/ai.js:399`, support at `shared/ai.js:483`, engineering at `shared/ai.js:500-502`, and the remaining retreat/move/assault commands at `shared/ai.js:802-805`. `hands` counts selected march decisions at `shared/ai.js:445-450`; it is neither a command cap nor an APM budget. | Put every player-seat path through one input executor. Buying, abilities, stance changes, engineering, aircraft and persistent-operation commands must all pay input time. |
| 2. The first look acts immediately. | Confirmed when a look is invoked, not as a universal tick-zero claim. There is no opening gate between `shared/ai.js:230` and `shared/ai.js:399`. Live invocation is staggered by `(g.tick + i * 13) % every` at `server.js:610`, after a delivered observation exists. `every` is 120/40/20 ticks at `shared/ai.js:220-222`. `handToAi()` resets memory and observation at `server.js:378-384` but sets no takeover delay. `opts.handoff` only clears an assault at `shared/ai.js:306`; it does not prevent purchases or other actions. | Measure first accepted input from match start and takeover separately. Add an opening look and a takeover look independent of the existing modulo phase. |
| 3. Every visible unit receives detailed simultaneous perception. | Confirmed inside team vision, not outside fog. `viewFor()` obtains the human snapshot at `shared/ai-view.js:128`, decodes every row into type, health, suppression, cooldown and cover at `shared/ai-view.js:9-17`, then defines all delivered enemies as visible at `shared/ai-view.js:158`. `plan()` collects all of them at `shared/ai.js:315`. No camera filters these details. These are detailed snapshot values, not unrounded authoritative state: snapshots round position and suppression and ceil health (`shared/sim.js:4576-4578,4730`). | Derive screen detail from a camera before planning. Keep team-wide coarse signals and earlier screen sightings separate. Preserve the existing fog boundary. |
| 4. Orders use computed points and independent per-unit destinations. | Confirmed with qualification: ordinary capture destinations already have random scatter, but it is tactical cover selection rather than mouse error. `spotNear()` picks an exact cover-cell center or a point 4 m from the objective at `shared/ai.js:136-144`; distinct units receive distinct computed targets at `shared/ai.js:717,738`. `operationPosition()` searches reachable delivered cells and scores cover, danger and line of sight at `shared/ai-mind.js:144-164`. Support uses exact enemy/cluster positions at `shared/ai.js:470,489-495`. Human `formation()` already produces several unit destinations from one clicked anchor at `client/main.js:1204-1208`, including trench snapping at `client/main.js:1210-1220`. | Scatter the intended screen click in pointer coordinates, then run the same formation and snapping rules as the client. Multiple destinations from a single formation click are valid; unrelated optimal destinations are not. |
| 5. Every fight gets micro while production continues. | Substantially confirmed. Purchases precede the combat loop at `shared/ai.js:399`; that loop visits all combat units at `shared/ai.js:590`, checks abilities at `shared/ai.js:596-611`, and retreats hurt units at `shared/ai.js:614`. Production has no attention contention with combat. Qualification: throws are capped per look at `shared/ai.js:454-457`, and Hard chooses at most one new focus target per look at `shared/ai.js:752`, not every fight's target simultaneously. Global focus scoring still inspects exact DPS, range, line of sight and enemy health at `shared/ai.js:743-760`. | Schedule attention by fight and base concern. Preserve useful micro but make competing concerns wait. |
| 6. Money is spent as soon as a look finds it affordable. | Confirmed when a desired purchase passes the command validator. Affordability is exact at `shared/ai.js:377`; purchases run unconditionally per look at `shared/ai.js:399`, with up to four buy commands for larger army settings. Economy reserves can deliberately defer spending (`shared/ai.js:298,397-399`), so zero resource float is not a universal claim. Classic building decisions also inspect exact resources at `shared/ai.js:831,836`. | Buying requires attending production and completing the input sequence. Measure resource float under combat load as well as buy latency. |
| 7. Matches use the same script. | Confirmed for opening decision families, qualified for literal trajectories. The unit wish chain is deterministic for the same observation and roster (`shared/ai.js:363-386`); Classic building priority is deterministic (`shared/ai.js:818-819`); point scoring and tie resolution are deterministic (`shared/ai.js:692-704`). There is no match persona. Movement cover and Flak placement already use unseeded randomness (`shared/ai.js:142-143,835`), so identical situations need not produce byte-identical command targets. Adaptive counters and loss learning also change later decisions. | Seed all randomness per seat, add plausible opening families, and score options with skill-dependent uncertainty. Count strategic opening families separately from coordinate jitter. |
| 8. It never forgets, idles, misjudges or hesitates. | The absolute claim is false. Enemy sightings expire after 60 s (`shared/ai-view.js:70`); contact notice records expire too (`shared/ai-mind.js:19`). Losses change respect and objective penalties (`shared/ai-mind.js:23-43`), and wait/commit behavior already exists (`shared/ai.js:339-351`). Incomplete fog knowledge permits surprise. Idle units can still remain idle when no valid target is chosen (`shared/ai.js:705`). | Add attention-dependent recognition of idle units, uncertain remembered strength and occasional decision pauses. Preserve existing commitment, loss learning and fog surprise instead of replacing them with a claim that the AI was infallible. |

## Additional issues and exceptions

- There is no selection cost. Orders use arbitrary ID lists, for example the cross-roster stance at `shared/ai.js:777`, all hurt units at `shared/ai.js:802`, and focus participants at `shared/ai.js:772`. Human group recall filters a stored group and double recall centers it at `client/selection.js:93-96`. The executor needs a current selection and stored groups.
- Several paths bypass even the existing march counter: aircraft at `shared/ai.js:555-561`, cut-point recovery at `shared/ai.js:716`, medics at `shared/ai.js:617`, and `planAssault()` output at `shared/ai.js:805`. Limit inputs at the common send boundary, not only at `takeHand()`.
- Support against an incoming plane skips contact notice and reacts wherever own units are threatened (`shared/ai.js:487-488`). This is an alert-like signal, but currently pays no alert recognition, camera switch or click latency.
- Own health, cooldowns, building progress and production queues are available globally (`shared/ai-view.js:9-17,40-46`). Perception rules must address own detail too. A recalled control group may enable orders without giving it current on-screen health.
- The AI mirrors accepted purchases, casts and stance changes into its local view during the same invocation (`shared/ai.js:246-270`). Delaying commands makes these projections stale. Planning must reserve pending spending and targets, then reconcile actual command results at execution.
- Horde movement is already spread across observation beats with `HORDE_ORDER_BATCH = 4` (`shared/ai.js:164,174-196`). That is a CPU/path-search budget, not a human input model. Keep the Wave director exception explicit and route co-op player seats through the ordinary commander.
- Benchmarks do not all use identical delivery semantics. `tools/bench.mjs:66` calls `think()` directly on a fixed Normal schedule; `tools/bench-engine.mjs:253-254` and `tools/ai-balance.mjs:60` use delivered views and difficulty periods; `tools/bench-world-conquest.mjs:148` includes a direct single-view call. Update each caller deliberately and retain a direct decision-layer test path.

## Human interface facts to preserve

The small minimap exposes owner color, position, selection and a coarse vehicle distinction: dot radius is 3.5 for vehicles and 2.6 otherwise (`client/main.js:1624-1626`). It also exposes announced support footprints (`client/main.js:1620-1622`). It does not draw unit health or exact type. The zoomed paper map is richer: it draws each unit's type symbol at `client/map-view.js:228-234`. A future wide-camera/paper-map mode may therefore legitimately recognize type symbols while still withholding health and cooldown detail. The brief's “dots only everywhere outside the screen” must not erase that human capability.

Minimap clicks also snap to nearby enemy and friendly IDs, buildings and house cells (`client/main.js:1570-1578`), then use the ordinary dispatcher (`client/main.js:1646`). Thus a coarse off-camera enemy-dot click can legitimately produce an attack command. It must not reveal the target's health, cooldown or exact type to the decision layer merely because the dispatcher can resolve its ID.

One right-click may produce several network commands for a mixed selection, such as garrison, fire-at and formation move at `client/orders.js:45-51`, or movement plus building rally (`client/orders.js:61-62`). The brief's one-command-per-tick rule is a deliberate stricter AI rule. Log physical inputs separately from network commands, and serialize any generated command bundle without counting it as multiple clicks.

Default camera settings are a 42-degree vertical field of view (`client/main.js:411`), distance 85, pitch 0.95 radians and yaw 0 (`client/main.js:1264`). At 1920x1080 on flat ground, ray-plane intersection gives a trapezoid with corners relative to the camera target approximately `(-79.954, -55.290)`, `(79.954, -55.290)`, `(45.513, 31.473)`, `(-45.513, 31.473)` in metres. This is a mathematical derivation from the source, not a browser measurement. Terrain, HUD coverage and camera rotation alter visibility. A rectangular approximation should be named as such; its bounding box contains ground the actual frustum does not show.

For reproduction, let `s = sin(0.95)`, `c = cos(0.95)`, `q = tan(42 degrees / 2)` and use normalized screen corner `(nx, ny)`. The ray direction is `(nx*q*1920/1080, ny*q*c-s, -ny*q*s-c)`, its ground-intersection parameter is `t = -85*s / (ny*q*c-s)`, and the ground coordinates are `(t*nx*q*1920/1080, 85*c+t*(-ny*q*s-c))`. This uses the camera pose at `client/camera.js:59-63`.

Normal pan speed at this zoom is 93.5 m/s, derived from `cam.dist * 1.1 * PAN[speed]` at `client/camera.js:276-277` and the default multiplier 1 at `client/camera.js:54,57`. Edge scrolling eases over 0.15 s (`client/camera.js:232,250-253`). Minimap clicks relocate the camera immediately once clicked (`client/main.js:1645`); Space relocates to the newest alert (`client/main.js:1331`). Charge recognition and input time for these jumps, not travel time proportional to map distance.

## Scope of evidence

The subsequent HUD audit found two further boundaries in the replacement. A cooldown in a delivered unit row is not an on-screen cooldown indicator: the human reads it in the selected-type HUD (`client/hud.js:385`). The replacement now masks unselected and enemy cooldowns, supplies aggregate group readings, and selects a squad through `queueInspection` before a meaningful ability decision (`shared/ai-perception.js`, `shared/ai-hands.js`, `shared/ai-commander.js`). The human world health bar supplies an estimate; the selected singleton HUD supplies rounded numeric health (`client/hud.js:292`).

Deleting the returned snapshot was also insufficient while its previous detector copy and projection sightings were reachable through planner memory. The raw detector baseline now lives in a private state-keyed WeakMap, and the fog adapter's memory lives in a separate private WeakMap (`shared/ai-perception.js:217`, `shared/ai.js:161`). Planner-memory traversal tests cover the neighboring denied detail, in addition to action-level invariance. These are corrections to the replacement, not claims about hidden fog access in the original AI.

This is a code audit, not a measured baseline. It proves the available paths and their synchronous nature. Accepted command counts, opening variety, effective APM and end-to-end reaction distributions belong in the baseline report from the measurement tool. Research and the provenance of every proposed difficulty number are in [the research note](research/human-like-rts-ai.md).
